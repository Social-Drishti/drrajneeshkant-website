Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Chiropractic treatment is a non-surgical approach that focuses on diagnosing and treating musculoskeletal disorders, particularly those related to the spine. It involves manual adjustment and manipulation of the spine to improve alignment, reduce pain, and enhance the body's natural ability to heal itself.

A typical session includes an initial assessment of your condition, physical examination, and then targeted spinal manipulation or mobilisation techniques. The practitioner may also include soft tissue therapy, postural advice, and specific exercises as part of your treatment plan.

You should consider physiotherapy if you are experiencing persistent pain, limited mobility, recovering from injury or surgery, or dealing with chronic conditions like back pain, neck pain, or joint problems. Early intervention often leads to faster and more complete recovery.

An initial consultation typically takes 45-60 minutes as it includes a full assessment. Follow-up treatment sessions usually last 30-45 minutes, depending on your condition and the treatment techniques being used.

The number of sessions varies depending on the severity of your condition, how long you've had it, and your body's response to treatment. Some patients feel improvement within 3-4 sessions, while more complex cases may require a longer treatment plan. Your practitioner will discuss a realistic timeline during your initial assessment.

Chiropractic care is suitable for most people, including the elderly and children. However, certain conditions may require modified approaches. During your initial consultation, the practitioner will conduct a thorough assessment to determine if chiropractic treatment is appropriate for your specific situation.

Bring any relevant medical reports, imaging results (X-rays, MRI), a list of current medications, and wear comfortable clothing that allows easy movement. If you have previous treatment records, those can also be helpful for the practitioner to understand your history.
