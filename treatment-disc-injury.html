Disc Injury Treatment

Non-Surgical Treatment for Spinal Disc Injuries

Herniated and bulging discs can cause severe pain, numbness, and limited mobility. The spinal discs act as cushions between the vertebrae, and when they are damaged, they can press on nearby nerves and spinal structures, leading to significant discomfort.

Non-surgical treatment offers effective relief and long-term recovery for many disc conditions. Through targeted chiropractic adjustments, spinal decompression therapy, and progressive rehabilitation, the pressure on affected discs and nerves can be reduced, supporting the body's natural healing process.

Common Types of Disc Injuries

Herniated Disc: Also known as a slipped or ruptured disc, this occurs when the inner nucleus of the disc pushes through the outer ring. It commonly affects the lumbar and cervical spine and can cause significant pain, numbness, and weakness.

Bulging Disc: A bulging disc occurs when the disc extends beyond its normal boundaries but remains intact. It can put pressure on surrounding nerves and cause pain that radiates into the arms or legs.

Degenerative Disc Disease: Natural ageing and wear can cause discs to lose hydration and height, reducing their ability to cushion the vertebrae. This can lead to chronic pain and reduced spinal function.

Trauma: Acute injuries from falls, accidents, or heavy lifting can damage spinal discs, causing immediate pain and potential nerve involvement.

Benefits of Disc Injury Treatment

Non-Surgical Approach: Chiropractic care and physiotherapy provide effective disc injury management without the risks and lengthy recovery associated with surgical intervention.

Pain Relief: Spinal decompression and targeted adjustments reduce the pressure on discs and nerves, providing meaningful pain reduction.

Improved Function: Treatment restores normal spinal mechanics, improving overall function and reducing the impact of the disc injury on daily activities.

Core Stability: Rehabilitation exercises strengthen the muscles that support the spine, providing better protection for the injured disc.
