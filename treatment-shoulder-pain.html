Shoulder Pain Treatment

Targeted Treatment for Shoulder Pain and Joint Problems

Shoulder pain can arise from a variety of conditions including frozen shoulder, rotator cuff injuries, impingement syndromes, postural problems, and arthritis. The shoulder is one of the most mobile joints in the body, and this mobility makes it particularly susceptible to injury and dysfunction.

Targeted physiotherapy and chiropractic care can effectively address shoulder pain by restoring joint mechanics, reducing inflammation, and rebuilding strength. Whether your shoulder problem is acute or chronic, a structured treatment plan can help you regain full, pain-free movement.

Common Causes of Shoulder Pain

Frozen Shoulder: Also known as adhesive capsulitis, frozen shoulder causes significant stiffness and pain in the shoulder joint. The condition develops gradually and can severely restrict arm movement over time.

Rotator Cuff Tear: The rotator cuff is a group of muscles and tendons that stabilise the shoulder. Tears can occur from acute injury or repetitive overhead activities, causing pain and weakness.

Impingement: Shoulder impingement occurs when the tendons of the rotator cuff are compressed between the bones of the shoulder during arm movements, leading to pain and inflammation.

Postural Problems: Poor posture, particularly rounded shoulders and forward head position, places uneven stress on the shoulder joint and surrounding muscles, leading to chronic pain.

Arthritis: Osteoarthritis and inflammatory conditions can affect the shoulder joint, causing pain, stiffness, and reduced range of motion.

Benefits of Shoulder Pain Treatment

Restored Mobility: Manual therapy and targeted exercises work together to restore the shoulder's full range of motion.

Pain Reduction: Treatment addresses the underlying cause of shoulder pain, providing lasting relief rather than temporary symptom management.

Strength Rebuilding: Progressive exercise programmes rebuild the strength and endurance of the shoulder muscles, improving joint stability.

Injury Prevention: Corrective exercises and ergonomic guidance help prevent future shoulder injuries and recurrence of symptoms.
